Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Yuma

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

What It Looks Like

  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • Water trails on the upper wall, starting at the ceiling corner
  • A musty smell in one room with no visible source
  • Paint that bubbles, blisters or peels away from the ceiling surface
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Above

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

How We Fix It

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Find the Source

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Make It Safe

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

Whatever is over the ceiling, a subfloor or attic decking, has typically been soaked as well. We check that level as well, because drying only the ceiling leaves moisture behind.

Older Ceiling Materials

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. Have a sample tested before any scraping, cutting or removal if your ceiling is one of these.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

Sudden Collapse

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. An entire piece can fall from a bulging spot onto anything below.
