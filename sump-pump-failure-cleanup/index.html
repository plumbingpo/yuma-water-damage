Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Yuma

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelySee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Finished Basement Walls

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Wall Framing

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Low Electrical Outlets

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Have an electrician look at wet receptacles and cable before power is restored to them.

Stored Belongings

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.
