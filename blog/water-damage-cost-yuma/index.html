How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Yuma?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Yuma?

The Short Answer

Most water damage jobs in Yuma fall somewhere between $1,500 and $10,000 or more. With clean water and a small area, the total can stay below about $1,500. Major flooding or a sewage backup that needs rebuilding can run well above $10,000.

Those numbers describe what is typical nationally; they are not a quote. Pricing in Yuma, AZ depends on local wages and the going rate for materials, so your number may differ. For a precise figure, there is no substitute for moisture readings taken during an on-site visit.

How Water Type Affects Cost

Restoration pricing usually starts with the type of water involved. Contamination level places every loss in one of three industry categories. The dirtier the water, the more protective gear, cleaning and material removal the job needs.

Nationally, the category puts cleanup at about $3 to $7.50 for each square foot. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1 (clean water)Supply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresRoughly $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Sewage, river flooding, storm surgeRoughly $7 to $7.50

The category is not fixed. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. That is one reason waiting costs money.

What Drives the Price

An identical pipe break in two houses can lead to bills that are far apart. When a job is priced, the contractor considers the following.

  • Area affected. More wet square footage means more labor, more equipment and more days on site.
  • Water category. Clean water is the cheapest to handle. The highest prices come with sewage.
  • How long it sat. Water spreads into subfloors and wall cavities over time. Catching a leak within an hour is much cheaper than discovering it when you return from a weekend away.
  • Materials. Drying goes smoothly on tile and on concrete. Hardwood, carpet pad, drywall and insulation often need special drying or replacement.
  • Mold. If growth has started, mold remediation is added to the scope, with containment and air filtration.
  • Reconstruction. The drying phase has its own price. Putting back cabinets, paint, flooring and drywall is billed separately, and for a big loss that part is frequently larger.

Most estimates are built around two phases: water extraction first, then structural drying. Plan on 3 to 5 days for the drying in most cases.

Many companies charge for each day the air movers and dehumidifiers run. A home that dries in three days costs less than one that takes six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

Below are the jobs our team handles most in Yuma, with the national range that is typical for each. They are a place to begin, and an actual price will differ.

  • Burst pipe. Often $1,500 to $5,000 for cleanup and drying, plus the burst pipe repair itself. Expect more if it ran for hours or soaked more than one floor.
  • Appliance leak. Catch a leaking water heater, washing machine or dishwasher early and the bill is often $1,000 to $4,000. Hidden damage makes a slow leak beneath a cabinet more expensive.
  • Basement flood. Depth, where the water came from and whether the room is finished put most jobs at $2,000 to $10,000 or more. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. Commonly $3,000 to $10,000 and up. Because sewage cleanup falls under Category 3, crews disinfect the whole area and take out porous materials.
  • Roof leak. Often $1,000 to $5,000 for drying and repairing ceilings, insulation and walls, not counting the roof fix.

Is Water Damage in Yuma Covered by Insurance?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Standard homeowners policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or a supply line that fails without warning.

Gradual leaks are usually not covered. If a fitting dripped for months, an insurer may call it a maintenance issue. Flood water from outside, such as a rising river or storm surge, is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood insurance. For sewer backup, a dedicated add-on is often required.

Check your policy or call your agent before you assume anything. The deductible is a factor as well. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

How to Keep the Bill Down

What happens early, within the first couple of days, determines the bulk of a water damage bill. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Until someone removes it, water continues to spread. Each additional hour soaks more of the building.
  • Shut off the water. Know where your main valve is and close it as soon as you find a leak.
  • Document everything. Shoot video and pictures first, while everything is still in place and nothing has been tossed. Save every receipt.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. A job with active growth takes more days and costs more money.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. Saving money is never worth an infection or an electric shock. Water that entered from outdoors calls for professional flood cleanup by people wearing the correct protection.

Getting a Real Number

National ranges are useful for planning, but your home is not an average. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.

Damage only gets bigger with time, so call for restoration of water damage in Yuma, AZ and have the inspection done now.
