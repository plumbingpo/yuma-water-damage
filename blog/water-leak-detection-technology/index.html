Leak Detection Systems Compared

At one end are basic standalone sensors, and at the other are comprehensive systems that cover the whole home. A point sensor is a small battery-powered unit that sits near a sink, washing machine, water heater, or other risk area and sounds an alarm if it detects water. Smart sensors join your WiFi network, so a moisture reading sends an alert to your smartphone even if you are not home. Installed on the main water line, whole-home systems track flow for anomalies, and a detected leak can trigger an automatic shutoff.

Where to Place Leak Detectors

The riskiest places, and so the best ones for a leak sensor, are the basement or sump pump area, utility rooms and crawl spaces containing plumbing, the HVAC condensate drain line, a refrigerator that is plumbed for water, the dishwasher, the washing machine (behind it), the water heater, and the cabinets beneath kitchen and bathroom sinks.

When Professionals Look for Leaks

When leaks are suspected but not visible, professional leak detection services use advanced technology to locate hidden water intrusion. Moisture under a floor or behind a wall changes the surface temperature, and thermal imaging cameras show that difference. Acoustic listening devices detect the sound of water flowing through hidden pipes. For precise moisture content numbers in building materials, technicians rely on moisture meters.
