Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Yuma

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Signs to Look For

  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Our Process

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.
