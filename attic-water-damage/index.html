Attic Water Damage in Yuma

Attic water damage comes from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In Yuma, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Rot Sets In

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Trace

We check the roof deck, flashing points, vents and exhaust ducts to learn whether the water is a leak, condensation, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. We protect the living space below and keep the access path contained.

3

Dry the Framing

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Fix the Cause

We point out the roof or ventilation problem that needs correcting and can share our findings with your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Bath Fans Venting Inside

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

Ceiling Collapse

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Mold on Sheathing

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

Air handlers, with their drain pans and condensate lines, sit in many attics. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.
