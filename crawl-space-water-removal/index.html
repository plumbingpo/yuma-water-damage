Crawl Space Water Removal in Yuma

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

Signs to Look For

  • More crickets, roaches or other bugs showing up near the floor level
  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Water That Is Not Clean

Water under a house is often not clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

What You Breathe Upstairs

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.

What We Do

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

How the Damage Progresses

Day one

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Save or Replace

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.
