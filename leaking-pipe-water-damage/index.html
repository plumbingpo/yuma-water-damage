Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Yuma

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. For Yuma homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. That is our part of the job. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Check the Water MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Read your policy and talk to your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Years of excessive water pressure straining valves and joints until one of them starts to weep.
  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • Shifting soil or abrasion wearing through water lines that run beneath a concrete slab.
  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Decayed Wall Framing

Wall studs and bottom plates that stay damp for weeks begin to soften and decay. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. Wet drywall softens and sags over time, and those sections often have to come out.

Flooring Damage

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.
