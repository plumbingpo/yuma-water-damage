Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Yuma: What to Know

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

After extracting the water, our Yuma crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Right Now

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWater from the tank can scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • A missing expansion tank or a failed thermostat that lets too much pressure build inside.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Platform and Subfloor

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.
