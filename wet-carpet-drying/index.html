Wet Carpet Drying in Yuma

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In Yuma, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • A rust colored line at the wall edge, a sign of wet tack strip
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
How We Fix It

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Furniture Marks

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.
