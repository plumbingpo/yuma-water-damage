Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Yuma crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. Each is its own phase of the job. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. Source control, so no new water enters. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Find the main water valve and make sure it has not seized.
  • Schedule an annual water heater inspection and swap the unit out before its tank gives way.
  • Once a month, look beneath sinks and near toilets for stains, drips or soft spots in the floor.
  • Leave the heat on during winter trips so the plumbing cannot freeze.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and roof penetrations before the next heavy rain arrives.
  • Install an automatic shutoff valve that closes the main when a leak is detected.

How We Handle Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Yuma Restoration Co:

1

Control the Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. Should a lasting fix require a plumber or a roofer, our team coordinates when that happens.

2

Remove Standing Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Contain Wet Areas

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

Why Water Damage Mitigation Is Not a DIY Job

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Under most policies you must act reasonably to keep the damage from growing, with proof that you did. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. That file shows what the water did and what was done about it. Whoever handles repairs then begins from a known condition, and nothing wet ends up sealed behind new wallboard.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • A musty odor has begun because materials stayed wet for a day.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • The space under your cabinets in the bathroom or kitchen took on water you cannot get to.
  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.
